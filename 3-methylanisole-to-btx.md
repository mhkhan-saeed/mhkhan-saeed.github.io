## The question

Today BTX is made by catalytic reforming of naphtha and from pyrolysis gasoline, then separated and sold into plastics, fibers, coatings, solvents and pharmaceuticals. A bio-based route could cut that dependence on crude oil.

</figure>

Lignin is the natural feedstock for aromatics, but cellulose and hemicellulose, the sugars bioethanol plants already process, have no direct thermochemical route to BTX. Fermentation offers a shortcut. Some strains of the yeast *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* produce phenolic molecules such as **3-methylanisole** from sugars, and continuous, scalable production has been demonstrated. If that molecule can be deoxygenated efficiently, an ethanol plant gains a second product stream of high-value aromatics. Almost no work exists on converting 3-methylanisole itself, and none at ambient hydrogen pressure over metal oxides.

## What I did

- **Screened 10+ MoO<sub>3</sub>-based catalysts**, including reduced bulk MoO<sub>3</sub> and zirconia-supported MoO<sub>3</sub>, for ambient-pressure HDO of 3-methylanisole.
- Characterized the catalysts (surface area, oxygen uptake, metal loading) and identified the full product slate by GC and GC-MS at different temperatures.
- **Mapped the reaction network** connecting 3-methylanisole to every product observed.
- Proposed an **integrated process** that pairs fermentation with HDO and hands the aromatic stream to existing petrochemical separations.

## What I found so far

- **MoO<sub>3</sub> can fully deoxygenate 3-methylanisole into aromatics** at the higher temperature tested, with toluene and xylenes as the main products.
- **Temperature and support tune the BTX split.** Changing the temperature, or moving from bulk to zirconia-supported MoO<sub>3</sub>, shifts the balance between toluene, xylenes and heavier aromatics. The supported catalyst favors xylenes and suppresses cracking to benzene.
- **Methylation is a big part of the chemistry,** which is useful because p-xylene, the feedstock for PET, is one of the most valuable products.

## What comes next

- A **support library** (ZrO<sub>2</sub>, Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>, TiO<sub>2</sub>, SiO<sub>2</sub>, H-ZSM-5) at different MoO<sub>3</sub> loadings, building on our [Sabatier descriptor work](research-sabatier-descriptors.html), to steer selectivity toward the most valuable aromatics.
- **Kinetic and mechanistic studies** of HDO and methylation over the best catalysts, paired with characterization.
- A **techno-economic analysis** to decide whether a bioethanol plant should run the HDO step itself or ship 3-methylanisole or crude BTX to a petrochemical site.

## Why it matters for industry

The route creates a new revenue stream for bioethanol and biogas plants and a bio-based supply of BTX that fits **existing bioethanol plants and petrochemical infrastructure**, with product selectivity tunable through catalyst design.
