## The question

When lignin is broken down, the aromatic monomers come with small aliphatic oxygenates such as ketones and aldehydes. Ideally these become C<sub>2</sub>–C<sub>4</sub> **alkenes**, which a steam cracker or polymer plant can use directly. Over molybdenum oxide, part of that stream instead ends up as lower-value **alkanes**. Earlier kinetics suggested the culprit is a competing C–O hydrogenolysis pathway rather than simple hydrogenation of the alkene. The question was whether the support underneath the MoO<sub>x</sub> could be used to tune that balance.

## My role

I synthesized and characterized a library of 15+ supported molybdenum oxide catalysts on Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>, Nb<sub>2</sub>O<sub>5</sub>, SiO<sub>2</sub>, TiO<sub>2</sub> and ZrO<sub>2</sub> at a range of MoO<sub>x</sub> surface densities, and worked on interpreting how their structure related to their activity and selectivity in short-chain carbonyl HDO.

## What we learned

- **Oxygen removal follows a Sabatier relationship.** The support's electronegativity and the degree of MoO<sub>x</sub> oligomerization together set the electron density of the Mo active site, and with it how strongly oxygen binds. Activity peaks at an intermediate binding strength.
- **Selectivity is the support's job.** The alkene-versus-alkane split is not governed by the MoO<sub>x</sub> structure but by the support, through its point of zero charge. That implicates the bridging Mo–O–support bond as the hydrogenolysis site.
- **The optimum depends on the molecule.** For less stable intermediates such as aldehydes, which are more relevant to lignin, the hydrogenolysis optimum shifts toward stronger binding on higher point-of-zero-charge supports.

## Why it matters for industry

Support selection sets the number of active sites and the selectivity; MoO<sub>x</sub> surface density then fine-tunes the intrinsic activity. Together that gives a practical recipe for balancing activity and alkene selectivity when designing catalysts for complex, lignin-derived streams.
