## What I work on

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Chemical Engineering at Iowa State University, advised by Prof. Brent H. Shanks and part of the NSF Center for Biorenewable Chemicals (CBiRC). My research is on hydrodeoxygenation (HDO): removing oxygen from lignin-derived and fermentation-derived molecules over molybdenum oxide catalysts at ambient hydrogen pressure, so that the products are aromatics and olefins that drop straight into existing petrochemical infrastructure.

I break catalyst design into four coupled problems, and my projects map onto them:

- **Adsorption.** Real feeds are mixtures. I developed a kinetic way to measure how strongly different oxygenates compete for the same active sites.
- **Selectivity.** I helped show how the oxide support steers a molybdenum oxide catalyst toward the products you want.
- **Stability.** I study why the catalyst loses activity, how to regenerate it, and how pretreatment might make it last longer.
- **Process viability.** I am testing a route from a bioethanol-plant byproduct to benzene, toluene and xylenes, and sketched how it could plug into an existing plant.

The details are on the [Research](research.html) page.

## How I like to work

I can own a catalyst across its development cycle: synthesis, screening, kinetic and selectivity testing, deactivation troubleshooting, and turning the results into a process decision. I build and run packed-bed microreactors, check that I am measuring chemistry and not mass transfer, and pair kinetics with characterization (XRD, XPS, chemisorption, electron microscopy) to explain what the rates are telling me.

## Before catalysis

Before graduate school I worked on the measurement and safety side of industrial chemistry. At BUET I analyzed water and soil samples from two transboundary river basins for a national water-quality monitoring study, and as an undergraduate I turned coconut husk into activated carbon for wastewater treatment. I then spent time in Health, Safety and Environment at Chevron Bangladesh, reviewing internal safety audits and writing the monthly safety briefings. Both experiences shaped how I treat method discipline and process safety: as part of the result, not an afterthought.

## Teaching and leadership

I have taught the undergraduate unit-operations laboratory (mass transfer and staged operations) and Environmental Chemodynamics at Iowa State, and served as Vice President and Professional Development Chair of the Chemical Engineering Graduate Student Organization.
